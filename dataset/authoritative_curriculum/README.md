# Authoritative Curriculum Reference: NCERT Class 10 Science (Physics)

## 1. Single Authoritative Source Rationale
In accordance with the project guidelines, **one primary textbook only** is adopted as the definitive baseline:
* **Textbook**: *Science: Textbook for Class X*
* **Publisher**: National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT), New Delhi, India
* **Edition**: Rationalised Curriculum (Chapters 9, 10, 11, and 12)
* **Accompanying Official Material**: *NCERT Exemplar Problems – Science Class X* and Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) Board Examinations Marking Schemes and Examiners' Reports.

### Why NCERT as the Sole Authority?
1. **Curricular Standardization**: The NCERT syllabus is the legal, national benchmark across CBSE and numerous state boards in India, serving over 3 million Class 10 candidates annually.
2. **Pedagogical Coherence**: It maintains strict internal consistency in:
   - Terminology (e.g., *New Cartesian Sign Convention*, *Conventional Current*, *Absolute Refractive Index*).
   - Sign conventions (e.g., positive direction along incident ray, object placed to the left).
   - Mathematical notations ($u, v, f, R, m, I, V, R, \rho, P, H$).
3. **No Competing Syllabus Conflict**: Using multiple textbooks from disparate educational jurisdictions (e.g., UK GCSE vs. US AP Physics vs. Indian NCERT) introduces contradictory conventions (such as real-is-positive vs. Cartesian coordinate sign conventions in optics). Adhering strictly to NCERT eliminates cross-jurisdiction convention pollution while retaining universal physics validity.
